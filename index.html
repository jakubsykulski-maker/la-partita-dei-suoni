The Mission

He does not need Polish grammar. He needs a reliable mouth-mechanic system: see letters, decode sounds, read out loud, make the kid smile. Every training block is built like football practice: repeat, slow down, speed up, then perform.

Rule 1
Polish spelling is consistent. Trust the letters.
Rule 2
Italian vowels are an advantage. Keep them clean.
Rule 3
No phantom vowels inside clusters. Szczecin is not "se-che-cheen".

First Half: No Phantom Vowels

Week 2 · clusters · the hard part
Pressure

Italian wants to insert tiny vowels between consonants. Polish says no. This week trains compression: every consonant gets touched, but nothing extra appears.

Drills

Slow Motion VAR

Stretch the cluster for 3 seconds: s-z-cz. Then compress it over 5 repetitions.

No Extra Touches

Read "prz, krz, trz, szcz" ten times. If a vowel appears, restart the attack.

Stadium Echo

Say the word once quietly, once normally, once like a chant. Same sounds, more confidence.

Half-time score He should now read common Polish words with clusters without stopping every two letters.
3

Second Half: Real Sentences

Week 3 · rhythm · reading aloud
Flow

Now the training becomes dad-shaped: fewer isolated words, more bedtime-story rhythm. Meaning is optional. Flow matters.

Materials that work

Polish Picture Books

Pick pages with short sentences and repeat them until the mouth stops arguing.

Forvo / TTS Check

Read first, then listen. The correction sticks better after an attempt.

Football Headlines

Use Polish sports headlines for daily rhythm practice. Familiar topic, lower stress.

Daughter Test

If the story sounds warm and steady, small pronunciation mistakes are fine.

Match plan
Days 15-17
  • Read 3 short sentences
  • Repeat the same text tomorrow faster
Days 18-19
  • Read one paragraph
  • Mark every difficult cluster
Days 20-21
  • Read without preparation
  • Keep going after mistakes
Week 3 whistle He can now read unseen Polish sentences aloud at a natural bedtime pace.

Trophy Room

After four weeks, the win condition is simple: he can open a Polish children's book, decode the sounds, and read aloud with enough clarity and warmth that the story works.

  • Juventus advantage: discipline, repetition, match mentality.
  • Lech advantage: Polish words, Poznan energy, the local boss level.
  • Dad advantage: the audience already wants him to win.
